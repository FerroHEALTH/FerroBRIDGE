     THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION,
     Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union,
     Having regard to Regulation (EU) 2025/327 of the European Parliament and of the Council of
     11 February 2025 on the European Health Data Space and amending Directive 2011/24/EU
     and Regulation (EU) 2024/28471, and in particular Article 15(1) thereof,
     Whereas:
     (1)    Regulation (EU) 2025/327 aims to improve natural persons’ access to and control over
            their personal electronic health data in the context of healthcare and to facilitate the
            exchange of such data. To this end, Article 15(1) of Regulation (EU) 2025/327
            empowers the Commission to lay down the technical specifications for the priority
            categories of personal electronic health data, setting out the European electronic health
            record exchange format (EEHRxF).
     (2)    Commission Recommendation (EU) 2019/243(2) sets out a framework for the
            development of the EEHRxF based on the eHealth Network guidelines on electronic
            exchange of electronic health data. That framework on the EEHRxF was further
            developed through the Xt-EHR joint action(3) funded under the EU4Health
            programme and through the eHealth Network. The development of the EEHRxF has
            also benefited from the technical and methodological work carried out under Horizon
            Europe-funded projects, in particular XpanDH and xShare, including
            recommendations on interoperability, implementation methodologies, feasibility
            demonstrations and stakeholder involvement.
     (3)    The EEHRxF is intended to facilitate the exchange of personal electronic health data
            referred to in Article 14(1) of Regulation (EU) 2025/327. The EEHRxF should be used
            in several cases, namely the download of an electronic copy of personal electronic
            health data when exercising the right of access, the transmission of a downloaded
            electronic copy to healthcare providers, the transmission of personal electronic health
            data between healthcare providers in different Member States following a natural
            person’s request when exercising the right to data portability, and the implementation
            of the harmonised interoperability software components of electronic health record
            systems (‘EHR systems’). The EEHRxF does not require any particular internal
            architecture or data storage model in EHR systems. It does not establish a minimum or
            maximum set of data that healthcare providers are required to register.

     (4)   Where personal electronic health data is collected and recorded in an EHR system, that
           data should be made available in the EEHRxF either upon issuance or transmission, to
           the extent that the data collected correspond to the data elements and use cases set out
           in this Regulation. For the purposes of this Regulation, the transmission of personal
           electronic health data may take place by sending the data or by making the data
           available in response to a request. References to access, download and transmission in
           this Regulation concern electronic means and do not include the provision of data on a
           physical medium or through postal or courier services.
     (5)   To understand, correctly identify and use personal electronic health data, it is
           necessary to provide information describing that data to query and retrieve the data in
           the context of an electronic exchange. Such information, commonly referred to as
           metadata, does not form part of the clinical content itself, but provides essential
           context, such as how the data can be identified, organised or related to other
           information. The EEHRxF should therefore include common metadata specifications
           applicable to the exchange, search and retrieval of priority categories of personal
           electronic health data.
     (6)   Information related to natural persons used in the EEHRxF, in particular in the
           metadata for query and retrieval, should be consistent, where relevant, with the
           healthcare attributes referred to in Article 2(1) of Commission Implementing
           Regulation (EU) 2026/2099, and with other attributes of natural persons issued to their
           European Digital Identity Wallets in accordance with Regulation (EU) No 910/2014.
     (7)   To enable the consistent and interoperable exchange of personal electronic health data,
           the EEHRxF should include technical specifications governing how such data are
           represented and exchanged. The EEHRxF should support the exchange of priority
           categories of personal electronic health data in both structured and unstructured form.
           Where personal electronic health data are not structured, the possibility of exchanging
           unstructured data, in the form of human-readable records, including text or portable
           document format (PDF) attachments, is necessary to safeguard patient safety and
           ensure continuity of care. The progressive increase of the share of structured data in
           health data exchanges including exchanges through national contact points for digital
           health, should further improve the usability of exchanged data.
     (8)   Considering the widespread use of the Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources
           developed by Health Level Seven International (‘HL7 FHIR’), the Digital Imaging and
           Communications in Medicine standard defined by the National Electrical
           Manufacturers Association (‘the DICOM standard’) and PDF in existing digital health
           infrastructures, these technical interoperability standards should form the technical
           basis of the EEHRxF. Version R4 of HL7 FHIR should be used as part of technical
           interoperability and exchange specifications, considering its maturity. The use of these
           standards in the EEHRxF should not prevent Member States from using other
           technical interoperability standards or information models within their national digital
           health infrastructures. Where other standards, information models or versions of HL7
           FHIR are used, Member States should ensure, through appropriate mappings or
           transformation mechanisms, that it remains possible to exchange personal electronic
           health data and to make that data available in conformity with the EEHRxF for the use
           cases set out in this Regulation.
     (9)   The priority categories of personal electronic health data covered by the EEHRxF
           differ in their purpose of exchange, content and level of clinical detail. Category-
           dependent specifications should determine, for each priority category of personal

            required structural format and semantic representation, taking into account the specific
            characteristics of that category. Where appropriate, the category-dependent
            specifications should rely on common data groups or elements whose meaning is
            determined in a uniform way in the EEHRxF.
     (10)   Some data groups are relevant to multiple priority categories of personal electronic
            health data. This includes shared clinical or administrative concepts, such as those
            relating to a natural person or a medicinal product. To avoid unnecessary duplication,
            inconsistencies in structure or meaning, and reduced interoperability, those data
            groups should be determined once and reused in category-dependent specifications,
            where appropriate, and be maintained accordingly.
     (11)   Semantic interoperability in the exchange of personal electronic health data requires
            the consistent interpretation of coded information across Member States. Where, to
            support the provision of healthcare services at national level, a Member State requires,
            for a specific data field, the use of a national or international coding system that is not
            referred to in binding requirements, mapping between coding systems is necessary to
            facilitate the implementation of EEHRxF use-cases. Where mapping is not possible,
            providing a textual designation in English facilitates the interpretation of the
            information. Consistent governance of value sets at Union level is necessary to ensure
            their uniform application across Member States.
     (12)   To assist the implementation of the EEHRxF, the Commission, in cooperation with the
            Member States, and taking into account the input of relevant stakeholders, should
            develop an EEHRxF implementation guide on the basis of this Regulation. To
            facilitate compliance with this Regulation, the EHDS Board should endorse the
            EEHRxF implementation guide. The Commission should publish the endorsed
            implementation guide, and information on its updated versions and their applicability
            periods. That information should clearly indicate whether a particular requirement in
            the EEHRxF implementation guide is affected by any updates of the EEHRxF. To
            facilitate the systematic updating of the EEHRxF implementation guide, the
            Commission, together with the EHDS Board, should develop and make publicly
            available an EEHRxF maintenance plan.
     (13)   [Article: National profiles] To take due account of national specificities in the
            organisation of healthcare services, Member States should be allowed to define
            national profiles of the EEHRxF, including for the operation of national digital health
            infrastructures. Such national profiles may provide additional requirements or
            restrictions for national use, including constraining or expanding the data groups, data
            elements, cardinalities, coding systems, value sets, data types and metadata. National
            profiles should not undermine national and cross-border interoperability by preventing
            the exchange, access, interpretation or processing of personal electronic health data
            conforming to the EEHRxF for use cases covered by this Regulation.

                                                   Article 1
                                           Subject matter and scope
     1.       This Regulation lays down the technical specifications for the priority categories of
              personal electronic health data referred to in Article 14(1) of Regulation (EU)
              2025/327, setting out the European electronic health record exchange format
              (EEHRxF).
     2.       The EEHRxF is intended to be used in the following cases:
              (a)   to download an electronic copy of personal electronic health data by natural
                    persons pursuant to Article 3(2) of Regulation (EU) 2025/327;
              (b)   to transmit a downloaded electronic copy of personal electronic health data to a
                    healthcare provider of the natural person’s choice pursuant to Article 7(4) of
                    Regulation (EU) 2025/327;
              (c)   to transmit personal electronic health data between healthcare providers
                    pursuant to Article 7(2) of Regulation (EU) 2025/327;
              (d)   to exchange personal electronic health data across borders through
                    MyHealth@EU pursuant to Article 23(3) of Regulation (EU) 2025/327;
              (e)   to implement the harmonised software components of EHR systems pursuant
                    to the common specifications provided for in Article 36 of Regulation (EU)
                    2025/327.

                                                     Article 2
                                                    Definitions
     For the purposes of this Regulation, the following definitions shall apply:
     1.       ‘structured content representation’ means a form of machine-readable encoding of
              personal electronic health data that enables automatic identification of individual
              elements;
     2.       ‘unstructured content representation’ means a form of encoding of personal
              electronic health data in a machine-readable transmission container, in the form of
              human-readable content, which does not enable automated identification of
              individual elements;

                                          Article 3
                    Technical interoperability and exchange specifications
     1.    Where the EEHRxF is used for the exchange of personal electronic health data, the
           exchange shall be performed by one or more of the following technical
           representations:
           (a)   a representation consisting of a portable document format (PDF) attachment
                 embedded in Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources developed by Health
                 Level Seven International (‘HL7 FHIR’) resource, including the applicable
                 metadata set;
           (b)   a representation compliant with HL7 FHIR and consisting of a set of human-
                 readable summaries expressed as a narrative, organised as per the relevant data
                 groups and including the applicable metadata set;

             (c)   a representation compliant with HL7 FHIR and consisting of structured data
                   accompanied by human-readable summaries of the same data expressed as a
                   narrative, organised as per the relevant data groups and including the
                   applicable metadata set;
             (d)   for medical imaging, a representation consisting of structured data using the
                   Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine standard defined by the
                   National Electrical Manufacturers Association (‘DICOM standard’), with

                   HL7 FHIR or the DICOM standard.

             version R4 of HL7 FHIR shall be used.

     The specifications for each priority category of personal electronic health data referred to in

                                              Article 6
                                    Coding systems and value sets
     1.      The coding systems to be used for each coded data field are set out in Annexes III to
             X. Where the binding requirement designates a preferred coding system among
             multiple options, that coding system shall be used, unless the concept to be
             represented is not covered by it. In that case, another coding system listed in the
             binding requirement shall be used. Where a Member State requires, for a coded data
             field, the use of a coding system that is not referred to in the binding requirements for
             that data field, it shall make available to the Commission and to the EHDS Board the
             mapping rules for at least one of the coding systems referred to in the binding
             requirements for that data field, using as the first choice the preferred coding system.
     2.      Member States shall ensure that such mapping rules are clinically validated,
             maintained and updated. Such mapping rules shall specify their input and output
             sides, the versions of the source and target coding systems, the degree of semantic
             equivalence, the provenance of clinical validation and any conditions or limitations
             of use.
     3.      Following the application to personal electronic health data of such mapping, both
             the original coding and the result of the mapping shall be provided as part of the
             coded data field. Where mapping is not possible and the original coding is not in
             English, the original national coding, together with its textual designation in English,
             shall be provided.

                                             Article 9
          Governance, implementation guide and versioning of the EEHRxF and maintenance
                                               plan
     1.        The Commission shall publish and maintain online the EEHRxF logical information
               models that represent the data structures set out in Annexes III to X.
     2.        The Commission, in cooperation with the Member States and taking into account the
               input of relevant stakeholders, shall develop and maintain the EEHRxF
               implementation guide on the basis of this Regulation, with a view to enabling the
               technical implementation of the EEHRxF. The EEHRxF implementation guide and
               any updates to it shall be endorsed by the EHDS Board. The Commission shall
               publish the first version of the EEHRxF implementation guide in machine-readable
               format by 31 October 2027.
     3.        The implementation guide referred to in paragraph 2 may adapt the names of data
               fields and data groups where necessary to comply with the naming conventions of
               the technical standard used for content representation. Such adaptations shall
               preserve the meaning, cardinalities and binding requirements of the elements
               concerned, as set out in this Regulation. The implementation guide shall include a
               correspondence between the elements set out in the Annexes and their technical
               representation.
     4.        The Commission shall make publicly available information on the versions of the
               EEHRxF and the EEHRxF implementation guide, including references to the
               applicable logical information models. That information shall identify which

                                             Article 10
                           Relation of the EEHRxF with national profiles
     1.       Member States may define national profiles of the EEHRxF, including for the
              purposes of operating national digital health infrastructures.
     2.       National profiles may introduce additional requirements or restrictions for national
              use, provided that those requirements or restrictions do not prevent the exchange,
              access, interpretation or processing of personal electronic health data conforming to
              the EEHRxF for the use cases referred to in Article 1(2).

                                               Article 11
                                            Entry into force
     This Regulation shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in
     the Official Journal of the European Union.
     This Regulation shall be binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States.
